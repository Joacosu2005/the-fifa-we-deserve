She has seen the game from every side

Most football officials learned the game from the boardroom. Klaveness learned it on the pitch, in the courtroom and at the federation table.

2002 to 2011

Player

73 caps and 9 goals for Norway, including the 2007 Women's World Cup in China, where Norway finished fourth.

Sat on the board of NISO, the Norwegian players' union.

Law career

Judge

Qualified as a lawyer while still playing. Worked as an assistant judge at the Oslo district court and as a special adviser to Norway's central bank.

Since March 2022

President

President of the Norwegian Football Federation and a member of UEFA's Executive Committee.

Between those roles she became the first Norwegian woman to commentate on men's football on TV, then spent four years as the federation's director of elite football, investing in academies and grassroots clubs.

As president, she backed that grassroots work, and Norway's men returned to the World Cup in 2026 after 28 years away, reaching the quarter-finals.

Three weeks after she was elected, she stood up at the FIFA Congress in Doha and said what many federations would not.

The Doha speech

Doha, 31 March 2022

"FIFA must act as a role model."

235 days before the Qatar World Cup, she told the FIFA Congress that the 2010 hosting decisions were made "in unacceptable ways with unacceptable consequences", and asked FIFA to care for the workers who built the tournament.

"The migrant workers injured, the families of those that died in the build-up to the World Cup must be cared for."FIFA Congress, Doha, 31 March 2022

Why FIFA needs a reset

This isn't about one person. These are the decisions that led six Nordic federations to call for a change of leadership in August 2026.

A peace prize with politics attached

FIFA created a Peace Prize and gave it to US President Donald Trump at the World Cup draw. The NFF backed an ethics complaint saying it breaks FIFA's own duty of political neutrality.

Reported by AP and Reuters

A rule bent mid-tournament

After Trump called FIFA's president, Folarin Balogun's automatic one-match ban at the 2026 World Cup was put on hold. The NFF asked FIFA to admit it was a mistake.

Reported by AFP, AP and Reuters, July 2026

The World Cup put up for sale

The FIFA Forward Enterprise plan would have sold a minority stake in World Cup commercial rights to outside investors. It was withdrawn after a UEFA boycott.

Reported by Reuters

Watchdogs that aren't seen as independent

FIFA's ethics process has faced questions since its lead investigator and judge were replaced in 2017. In 2026 the NFF filed complaints with both the Ethics Committee and the audit and compliance committee.

Reported by AP

Four rules for a FIFA people can trust

Football runs on the Laws of the Game. FIFA should run on rules too. These are the reforms our campaign puts at the centre of her platform.

1

Rule 1: Transparency

Publish how big decisions are made, who made them and who benefits, from hosting votes to commercial deals.

"Our members question ethics in sport and demand transparency."FIFA Congress, Doha, 31 March 2022
2

Rule 2: Accountability

The president answers to the member associations and the committees, and owns mistakes in public.

"We need our leader in FIFA to say that this was an error."The Times, July 2026
3

Rule 3: Independence

Ethics and disciplinary bodies work free from presidential and political pressure, with published reasoning for their verdicts.

"When you bend a rule like this, you're on a slippery slope that will put the whole game at risk."The Times, July 2026
4

Rule 4: Open finances

Independent audits, open commercial agreements and development money shared by clear criteria, not by loyalty.

"To try to sweep this under the carpet now will not fly."AFP, July 2026

From Doha to Rabat

Four years of asking FIFA hard questions, and the dates that decide what happens next.

  1. Mar 2022

    Elected NFF president

    Wins the presidency of the Norwegian Football Federation.

  2. 31 Mar 2022

    The Doha speech

    Tells the FIFA Congress the 2010 World Cup awards were made in "unacceptable ways" and that FIFA must be a role model.

  3. Apr 2026

    Calls to scrap the Peace Prize

    Says awarding peace prizes is not FIFA's role.

  4. Jul 2026

    Norway's World Cup, and the Balogun ruling

    Norway reaches the quarter-finals. Klaveness says the decision to suspend Balogun's ban "should not have happened".

  5. Aug 2026

    Two formal complaints and a call to resign

    The NFF board unanimously files complaints with FIFA's Ethics Committee and its audit and compliance committee. She calls on Infantino to resign; Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Iceland and the Faroe Islands join her.

  6. 28 Oct 2026

    She says she won't stand

    She tells The Guardian a candidacy is "not really a topic" for her and that Europe needs a candidate who can win. This campaign imagines she changes her mind.

  7. 18 Nov 2026

    Candidate deadline

    Candidates must register with the backing of five member associations.

  8. 18 Mar 2027

    The vote in Rabat

    211 member associations elect FIFA's president for 2027 to 2031.

Questions

Is Lise Klaveness running for FIFA president?

No. In October 2026 she said a candidacy was not on her agenda and that Europe should back a candidate who can win. This is a student concept campaign that imagines what her candidacy could look like.

Who votes in a FIFA election?

FIFA's 211 member associations, one vote each, at the FIFA Congress. Fans don't vote, which is why public pressure on national federations matters.

What does a candidate need to stand?

A declaration by 18 November 2026, backed by at least five member associations. The election takes place in Rabat, Morocco, on 18 March 2027.

What happens to my details if I sign?

About this page

The FIFA We Deserve is a concept marketing campaign created by students at IE Business School. It imagines Lise Klaveness as a candidate in the 2027 FIFA presidential election. She has not declared a candidacy, and in October 2026 she said she does not plan to.

We are not affiliated with Lise Klaveness, the Norwegian Football Federation, UEFA or FIFA. Quotes are taken from published reporting, linked here. Campaign reforms are our framing of positions she has stated publicly. Photos are used for this class project only; rights belong to their photographers.
